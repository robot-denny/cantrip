# 6.1 — the setup skill

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Gate:** 10/10. Slot coverage: 18 of 18 core slots addressed; 4 pack slots correctly *not* named.
**Spec:** the canary's four filled slot files, verbatim, plus its detection-difficulty gradient.

## The canary's gradient is the whole design

Their most useful contribution was not the slot contents — it was observing that **the slots sort into
tiers by how you get them**, and that the leverage is in the middle tier I had not articulated:

| Tier | Slots | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Detectable** | branch naming, commit format and trailers, build, tests, code layout, generated output, workspace | Read from the repo. Fill, then show for confirmation |
| **Written down but not detectable** | planning gotchas, implementation rules, unit of work, memory, reviewer orientation | **Mine the guidance file, runbooks, and agent memory** |
| **Genuinely unknown** | whatever remains | Ask, one question at a time |

Their observation that **planning gotchas were not auto-detectable at all** is the load-bearing one.
Those were hard-won platform quirks — a validator that rejects wildcards, a collision only `publish`
surfaces, a build container without a package manager — invisible to a fresh clone and only present
because someone had written them into a guidance file after being bitten.

"Detection over interrogation" was already a stated principle, but I had read it as a binary: detect, or
ask. **The middle tier is where the high-value slots live**, and mining prose someone already wrote is
neither detection nor interrogation. Naming it changed the spell's shape entirely.

One small addition of my own that follows from it: **prefer the CI or hook command over the README's.**
A README drifts; a hook is executed.

## Their four edges, resolved

**The remotes fact was a "genuine stretch."** They filed "never push to this remote" under
`## Branch naming`. I considered a new heading and declined — *no core spell pushes*, and by their own
reasoning about secrets, a slot nothing reads is documentation misfiled as configuration. Instead the
heading's scope now explicitly covers branch conventions *and which remotes are pushed to*, so it is no
longer a stretch. Cheaper than schema, and honest.

**Pack facts in a core-only interview.** Resolved simply: **write the fact under its correct pack heading
even when the pack is absent.** An unread heading costs nothing and is already right when the pack lands;
misfiling it under a core heading is harder to find later. The install checker already only surveys pack
slots when their pack is present, so nothing nags.

**`## Planning gotchas` had become a Cloud/CI catch-all.** Rather than split it, I tightened its stated
scope: constraints a plan must *satisfy*, explicitly **not operational topology** — which environment
deploys where, how promotion works, who restarts what. That is runbook material, and folding it in turns
one slot into something a planner reads past. Applied in both places that declare the slot.

**Scope of the interview.** Their question — capture the whole project, or only what the spells read? —
answered decisively as **only what the spells read**, and stated in the spell. Their secrets example is
exactly right: real, load-bearing, and correctly absent, because nothing reads it from a slot. Setup now
says where such facts belong instead of inventing a heading.

## Two things my own coverage check caught

I wrote a check comparing every `**Slot:**` declared anywhere against what `/setup` addresses. It found
**nine unaddressed slots** — the entire reviewer-rules family and all four pack slots. A careful read of
the spell would not have found those; nothing looks missing when it is missing.

Then adding the pack slots **broke check 8**, because `/setup` is L0 core and I had written pack heading
names into it. The right fix was the ADR 0003 pattern I had already established and again failed to reach
for: **core must not know a pack's slot names.** Setup now *reads the installed pack's skills and collects
their slot declarations*, which works because every skill declares its slots in the same form — the
configuration surface is self-describing.

That is now the fourth time a mechanism existed in one place and I did not carry it to a sibling. It is
the single most recurring defect class in this project, and the pattern is consistent: **I reach for the
mechanism when writing the thing it was invented for, and not when writing the next thing that needs
it.**

## Spell count

Ten core spells: eight workflow, plus `update-toolkit` and `setup` — maintenance and onboarding rather
than workflow stages. The 6–8 guidance was about the workflow set, which is still eight and unchanged
since Checkpoint C. Worth restating at Phase 7 rather than quietly letting the number drift.

## Carried forward

- **Untested.** Every prior increment could at least be exercised structurally; this one cannot, because
  running it requires an unconfigured project. The canary is now configured, so testing it means a second
  consumer or a scratch clone. **Its detection tier is the testable part** and worth exercising against a
  fresh clone of any repo before the pilot relies on it.
- The greenfield branch — "recording *not established yet* is the correct answer" — is the ADR 0006 rung-3
  path, and remains unexercised until a project with no conventions runs it.
